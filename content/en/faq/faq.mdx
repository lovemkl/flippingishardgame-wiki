## What Is Flipping is Hard?

A Foddian-style rage platformer by Elegant Horse Studios, a studio out of Vilnius, Lithuania. You play Flippy — a '90s flip phone lost in the land of lost things — climbing back to his owner, Jeff, using rotation as your only movement. Ten surreal areas, solo or with up to 32 players in online co-op. The complete guide covers the whole orientation; the short version is that the title is both a warning and a promise.

## Is It Too Hard for Me?

The honest answer: it is hard — that is the genre and the title — but its difficulty is the fair kind. Every fall has a nameable cause, nothing is gated behind reflexes you cannot train, and the players who summit are the ones who kept flipping, not the ones born with special hands. If you have ever enjoyed a game that made you angry and came back anyway, this is playable and worth it. ## How Do the Controls Work?

One verb: rotation. Flips build momentum, momentum moves you, landing angle decides whether you stick or slide. No jump button, no walking — Flippy is a phone, and phones tumble. The how-to-flip guide teaches the whole system properly; the short version is that the controls are simple to state and deep to own, which is the rage genre's signature trade.

## Does Co-op Make It Easier?

It makes it different rather than easier. Your flips remain your own — co-op populates the mountain rather than sharing your climb. What it changes: falls become witnessed (which is medicine), routes become visible (which is education), and the whole experience becomes social (which is either the point or the distraction, depending on the climber). The co-op guide covers the etiquette and the solo-versus-co-op question with a real answer.
